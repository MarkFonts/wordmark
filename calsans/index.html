Cal Sans for Cal.com

The family Cal.com keeps

Over five years WORDMARK has sharpened Cal.com’s typography, from one headline weight to the whole family. In the same span Cal.com raised $32M.

In 2021 Cal.com had no typographic DNA of its own, from logo to website to marketing. One weight proved a custom face could supplant a standard; the brand grew the rest.

Cal.com

Type “cal.com” and it reads as rings. The geometry Peer Richelsen, co-founder, requested for the redesigned logo from Calendso in 2021 is the geometry every weight keeps.

Geometry

Six axes in one file. Optical size, geometry, weight, ascender height, sharpness, and italic, each a slider the site CSS can move.

Open source from day one. Cal Sans lives on Google Fonts, npm, and jsDelivr, so anyone can ship it.

From the studio

I’m Mark Davis, and I run WORDMARK. The UI cut of Cal Sans was in round one; Cal.com took the headline first.

Five directions were on the table, from Futura in light disguise to a grotesk. Cal.com picked the one that reads as rings.

The other three were worth a shot.

The pull request to Google Fonts changed the pace. Peer saw the roadmap, asked if it was a UI font, and wanted to talk commercials that day.

“I went to the website and I immediately said, what’s that font? I don’t do that a lot. […] Rather than getting a logo design, they had a font, and they open sourced the font.”

One family. Three releases.

2021 · v1.000

Cal Sans

The geometric display weight, with six variants, Futura alternates, and historical ligatures. On Google Fonts.

2025 · v1.600

Cal Sans UI

Text proportions and spacing for product interfaces, variable on weight and GEOM. Shown off by the first cal.com/font page, built from code.

2026 · v2.000

Cal Sans 2

Display and text in one file, six axes, 1,550 glyphs. Installed in coss.ui and the site; on Google Fonts, npm, and jsDelivr.

One weight earned the trust

A startup doesn’t buy a font family, it buys a stronger brand. Cal.com trusted one weight to replace a standard, and every stage since served the brand it built.
