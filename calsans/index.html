Cal Sans for Cal.com

The family Cal.com keeps

Over five years WORDMARK has sharpened Cal.com’s typography, from one headline weight to the whole family. In the same span Cal.com raised $32M.

In 2021 Cal.com had no typographic DNA of its own, from logo to website to marketing. One weight proved a custom face could supplant a standard; the brand grew the rest.

How it came to be

Five directions, one deck.

The first presentation in 2021 set five ways to draw Cal.com. Direction 1, 1927, became the logo and the display face. Direction 4, human, was the text face in waiting.

Four masters became eight. The estimate proposed two display masters and two text masters, pitched as a family in the class of Uber Move, Airbnb Cereal and Pinterest Sans. On client trust it kept growing: Regular and Bold, at optical size 10 and 45, Roman and Italic. Eight drawings of every glyph, and thousands of brace layers between them carrying the geometry axis.

Inside the source.

One A, eight master layers named by their coordinates, and eight brace layers behind them carrying sharpness. Sixteen drawings per glyph is too many to nudge by hand, so the studio learned to script the adjustments across layers and keep the drawing in sync.

384 statics from one source.

calbuild runs the sources through fontmake, shifts the axis defaults, merges the feature variations and instances every static style, 384 of them, from the one variable file.

→Get the fonts

Tools built on the way. Not for want of features, for want of fewer: a proofer with the client’s own UI as its preview tabs and two optical sizes side by side in one paragraph panel, and nothing in the way. Four tools came out of that.

font-proofer ↗ The font in Cal.com’s real screens as preview tabs: booking pages, the app, the component board, and a paragraph panel that sets two optical sizes side by side.
ReCal ↗ Pick the defaults, bake them in, and download your own cut of Cal Sans from the browser. After DJR’s Input downloader and Belleve Invis’s Iosevka.
kernpare Every kerning pair across all eight masters at once, roman and italic optionally linked, with the curve a value takes from text to display. After Adobe’s Kernalytics, MIT.
calbuild ↗ The build, start to finish: metrics, validation, the variable font, the statics, the packages for Google Fonts, npm and jsDelivr. In the Cal Sans repo, under scripts.

From the studio

I’m Mark Davis, and I run WORDMARK. The UI cut of Cal Sans was in round one; Cal.com took the headline first.

Five directions were on the table, from Futura in light disguise to a grotesk. Cal.com picked the one that reads as rings.

The other three were worth a shot.

Matter cost about €1,400 a year. Inter cost nothing, and that was the problem; everyone has it.

The pull request to Google Fonts changed the pace. Peer saw the roadmap, asked if it was a UI font, and wanted to talk commercials that day.

Peer’s Cal Sans 2 post hit 60K views and 536 bookmarks in 12 hours, saved faster than it was liked.

“I went to the website and I immediately said, what’s that font? I don’t do that a lot. […] Rather than getting a logo design, they had a font, and they open sourced the font.”
